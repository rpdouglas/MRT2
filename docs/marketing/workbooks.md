## The one-sentence pitch

**My Workbooks turns real recovery literature — 12-Step, Recovery Dharma, or secular — into guided step work you actually do, one question at a time, with an AI coach reading alongside you.**

---

## Why this matters in recovery

- **Step work is a core recovery practice, not an invented exercise.** Structured written inventory is literally what "doing the Steps" means in AA/NA, and the same is true of Recovery Dharma's inquiry into the Four Noble Truths and Eightfold Path. Workbooks digitizes that real practice rather than replacing it with generic prompts.
- **One question at a time reduces overwhelm.** A full page of a workbook can be intimidating to open; a single question, with the actual literature passage it's grounded in shown right below it, is a much smaller ask — the same reason Journal offers templates instead of only a blank page.
- **AI coaching mid-answer is a real-time nudge, not a replacement for a sponsor.** It reads what someone's already written and offers a next question or reflection to go deeper — closer to "keep going" than a lecture.
- **Stepping back across a whole workbook (or a whole library) mirrors what a sponsor or counselor does across weeks of work** — spotting a pattern that isn't visible from any single answer. MRT's AI Compass does the same thing on demand, whenever someone's ready to see it.

**Marketing framing:** "real step work, made easier to actually sit down and do" — not a generic self-reflection app with a recovery theme painted on.

## What Workbooks actually does

### Four real recovery workbooks, ready from day one
- **General Recovery** — 25 direct questions, not tied to any one fellowship or tradition.
- **12-Step Workbook** — all 12 Steps, each opening with the Step's own text and grounded questions.
- **Recovery Dharma** — a Buddhist-informed path through the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path.
- **Women for Recovery** — eight themed sections addressing recovery through a woman's specific experience.

All four are already in someone's library from day one — nothing to unlock or purchase. A "manage your library" toggle lets someone hide any of them and bring them back later; **hiding a workbook never deletes its answers** — everything already written is kept and restored the moment it's re-added.

### One question at a time, not a wall of text
Opening a section drops into a focused, full-screen writing mode — one question, its grounding literature passage right below it, and nothing else competing for attention. Progress autosaves as someone writes, so there's nothing to remember to hit "save."

### An AI coach, right where you're writing
A per-question "AI Insight" button reads what's currently written and offers a next thought or a deeper question — grounded in the same literature context shown for that question, not a generic prompt.

### Consult the Recovery Compass — the whole-picture view
From a workbook's overview, "Consult the Recovery Compass" steps back and reflects on a chosen scope — one section, the whole workbook, or everything ever written across every workbook — and comes back with a summary, three distinct reflections (what's understood, what might be a blind spot, and where real growth shows), and concrete next steps that convert into tasks with one tap. Anything worth keeping can be saved to a private Wisdom Log.

### Honest progress, section by section
A visible completion percentage for each section and for the workbook as a whole — a real number, not a guess, and never framed as a deficit.

## How each persona uses Workbooks

MRT designs around six real recovery personas (full detail in `docs/PERSONAS.md`). Do not invent details beyond what's here or in the personas doc.

### Maya — "The Systematiser" (6–18 months, SMART Recovery / secular / CBT)
> *"I want to understand the mechanics of my brain so I can upgrade my operating system."*

Maya is Workbooks' primary audience. Her persona documentation names total questions answered — her "Wisdom Score" — as her primary engagement signal, and calls for linear, sequential progress with a visible completion percentage at all times. That's exactly this feature's shape.

**Marketing angle:** a real curriculum to work through, with a number that actually tracks how far she's come.

### Walt — "The Zen Master" (35+ years, AA-origin, now Recovery Dharma)
> *"Recovery is a lifelong practice of mindfulness and reflection."*

Walt's persona documentation names the Recovery Dharma workbook specifically as "his current programme's structured inquiry path" — not a generic feature fit, a named one. The option to step back and review across his whole library at once suits his long-horizon reflection style.

**Marketing angle:** his actual current practice, not an app's approximation of one.

### Ned — "The Pink Cloud" (Day 30–90, Narcotics Anonymous)
> *"I'm going to crush it today!"*

Workbooks isn't Ned's day-one tool, but his persona's own Day 90 transition plan explicitly calls for surfacing the first workbook sections as his next challenge right as the early-recovery momentum that carried him starts to fade — the same moment Journal's AI Pattern Analysis and the CBT toolset (`docs/marketing/tools.md`) are also introduced. All three arrive together, by design, at his most vulnerable inflection point.

**Marketing angle:** consistent with the rest of the series — the deeper thing waiting for him when the initial high fades.

### Lisa — "The Service Superstar" (7 years, Alcoholics Anonymous, sponsors 3–6 women)
> *"I have five sponsees and a full-time job. I need to stay organized so I don't burn out."*

The Women for Recovery workbook is a relevant resource for the population Lisa sponsors. **Be careful not to overclaim here:** there's no sponsor-sharing feature — Lisa can't see or share a sponsee's workbook answers from inside the app. If Lisa is mentioned, keep it to the workbook's relevance to the women she sponsors, not a shared-progress capability that doesn't exist.

### David and Jordan
Workbooks has no crisis-mode design and no MAT-specific adaptation — it's a deliberate, sit-down feature, not a crisis-floor one, and that's fine; not every feature needs a story for every persona. Don't force a David or Jordan angle here.

## Brand voice & marketing guardrails

Everything from `docs/marketing/journal.md`'s guardrails applies here too. Workbooks-specific additions:

- **Don't call the library a "marketplace" in a commercial sense.** There's no purchase flow and no third-party content — all four workbooks are included, free, for everyone. "Manage your library" or "choose your workbooks" is accurate; implying anything is bought or unlocked is not.
- **Don't claim sponsor-visible sharing.** Lisa cannot see a sponsee's workbook answers from inside the app today — no such feature exists.
- **Don't oversell the AI Compass as a therapist or sponsor replacement.** It reflects patterns in someone's own writing; it doesn't diagnose, counsel, or replace a real relationship with a sponsor or clinician.
- **Keep premium framing honest and non-specific.** There is a cost-control mechanism behind the AI features here, same spirit as Journal's — don't cite exact numbers (they can change), and don't claim premium is "unlimited" without qualification.
