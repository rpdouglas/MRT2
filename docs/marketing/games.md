# My Recovery Games — Marketing & Persona Brief

**What this document is for:** briefing material for an LLM writing marketing copy about MRT's Recovery Games — eight short, zero-shame games, each built around a real coping framework and a specific recovery moment. Grounded in the real, verified product, not generic "gamified wellness" language.

Seventh in the per-feature series (`docs/marketing/journal.md`, `dashboard.md`, `tools.md`, `tasks.md`, `vitality.md`, `workbooks.md` before this; `docs/marketing/README.md` tracks the rest). Built on `docs/screens/games/`, fully spot-verified against live source this pass (all 8 games plus the hub) — held up accurate throughout.

---

## The one-sentence pitch

**Recovery Games turns real coping tools — breathing rhythm, CBT patterns, the H.A.L.T. framework, momentum-building — into short games built for a specific moment, not generic gamification with a recovery theme painted on.**

---

## Why this matters in recovery

- **Each game is built on a real technique, not an invented mini-game.** Trigger Match uses H.A.L.T. (Hungry, Angry, Lonely, Tired) — a well-known relapse-trigger self-check used across both 12-Step and secular recovery. Thought Challenge uses the same 12-distortion cognitive-behavioral list the app's own guided CBT tools use, not a separate invented one.
- **Anti-shame isn't a slogan here — it shapes the mechanics.** Some games are built so there's literally nothing to break: Goal Ladder has no streak and no reset, because a broken streak would be actively harmful at the exact recovery moment it's designed for. Daily Crossword's score is always zero, on purpose — the puzzle is the point, not a number attached to it.
- **The content was actually reviewed for anonymity compliance, not just written and shipped.** Recovery Jeopardy's trivia pool went through a real Tradition-6 scrub — fellowship-specific references rewritten or removed, enforced going forward by an automated check. This is a real content-governance detail, not just a claim.
- **A deliberate privacy restraint, worth telling honestly.** One game (Trigger Match) was originally proposed to pull scenarios from a player's own journal history to feel more personal — and that idea was evaluated and declined specifically because it would have meant a game reading personal data it didn't need to. The content stayed static instead.

**Marketing framing:** "real tools, disguised as something you'd actually want to play" — not wellness-app trivia with a recovery label on it.

---

## What Recovery Games actually does

Eight games, each built for a specific person's moment:

- **Craving Buster** — a roughly 90-second breathing-rhythm tap game, reachable directly from the crisis (SOS) button — no unlocking anything first.
- **Recovery Jeopardy** — a real trivia board for 1–3 players or teams, pass-the-device, built for a sponsor/sponsee session or a home-group activity.
- **Fast Lane** — a multi-week life-management simulation: work, study, budget, build stability over real in-game weeks — the one game that saves continuously and picks up exactly where you left off, days or weeks later.
- **Goal Ladder** — eight small momentum-building prompts, one rung climbed per prompt. No streak, no reset — nothing for a missed day to punish.
- **Thought Challenge** — a CBT-style matching quiz built specifically for sponsor/service burnout, with an optional private reflection afterward.
- **Trigger Match** — pattern-recognition practice built on the H.A.L.T. framework.
- **Knowledge Quests** — short, bite-size psychoeducation packs (Stress & The Body, Habit Loops, Sleep & Recovery).
- **Daily Crossword** — one shared puzzle every day, themed around a recovery concept, deliberately unscored.

Every game is free, on every tier, with no exceptions.

### Progress that respects the moment
Most games keep a simple in-session score — never a public leaderboard, never shared without the player's own choice. Where scoring itself would work against the point (Daily Crossword, Goal Ladder), the game is built without one on purpose, not as an oversight.

## How each persona uses Recovery Games

This is the clearest 1:1 persona mapping anywhere in MRT — most games were built with one specific persona in mind, named directly in the product's own design documentation.

### David — "The Survivor" (Day 1–30, Cocaine Anonymous)
> *"I just need the noise to stop right now."*

**Craving Buster** is David's game — reachable straight from the SOS crisis flow, no PIN or unlock required, same crisis-tool precedent as Urge Surfer (`docs/marketing/tools.md`). A short, physical breathing-rhythm exercise for the exact moment an urge is happening, not a quiz to think through.

**Marketing angle:** the one game built to be reachable *during* a crisis, not just about one.

### Ned — "The Pink Cloud" (Day 30–90, Narcotics Anonymous)
> *"I'm going to crush it today!"*

**Goal Ladder** is Ned's game, and its design is a direct answer to his documented Day 90 risk: eight prompts, one rung each, no streak and nothing to reset. The anti-punishing design here doesn't come from forgiving math — it comes from there being nothing left to punish in the first place.

**Marketing angle:** built for the exact day most habit-trackers let someone down.

### Lisa — "The Service Superstar" (7 years, Alcoholics Anonymous, sponsors 3–6 women)
> *"I have five sponsees and a full-time job. I need to stay organized so I don't burn out."*

Lisa gets two games. **Recovery Jeopardy** is the one deliberately multiplayer, pass-the-device game in the app — built for exactly the kind of session she might run with a sponsee or her home group. **Thought Challenge** addresses her specific risk directly: service/sponsor burnout, not generic CBT for anyone.

**Marketing angle:** one game to play with someone else, one built for the specific way she burns out.

### Walt — "The Zen Master" (35+ years, AA-origin, now Recovery Dharma)
> *"Recovery is a lifelong practice of mindfulness and reflection."*

Walt also gets two. **Fast Lane** is the one game built for someone who thinks in the long term — a real multi-week simulation with genuine resumable state, not a five-minute distraction. **Trigger Match** offers quieter, reflective pattern-recognition practice using H.A.L.T.

**Marketing angle:** the one game in the set built to be played over weeks, for someone who's been at this for decades.

### Jordan — "The Stabiliser" (Day 1–12mo+, MAT/Buprenorphine or Naltrexone + MARA/SMART)
> *"My recovery combines medicine and behavior. I need tools that support my physical stability without judgment."*

No dedicated game exists for Jordan today — checked directly, none of the eight games carries her persona tag. Don't invent one in copy; if Jordan needs a mention, point to Tools or Vitality instead, both of which do have real content relevant to her.

### Everyone
**Knowledge Quests** and **Daily Crossword** aren't built for one persona — they're general psychoeducation and a shared daily ritual, open to anyone.

## How Recovery Games connects to the rest of MRT

- **Same reward system as Tasks** — game completions earn the same XP as completing a task, not a separate parallel points system.
- **Same encryption promise as everywhere else** — game data is included in the same privacy protections (PIN rotation, account-deletion crypto-shredding) as Journal and Workbooks.
- **Craving Buster and Urge Surfer are two different tools for the same crisis moment**, not duplicates of each other — one is a physical breathing-rhythm game, the other a static grounding checklist. Worth offering someone whichever fits how they're feeling in the moment.

## Brand voice & marketing guardrails

Everything from `docs/marketing/journal.md`'s guardrails applies here too. Games-specific additions:

- **Don't claim "no score, no streak, no timer" as a blanket statement.** Only Daily Crossword is genuinely scoreless — every other game keeps a simple in-session score. (The app's own hub screen makes this same overstatement in its footer copy — don't repeat it as if it applies to everything underneath.)
- **Don't invent a game for Jordan.** None exists — see her section above.
- **Don't oversell personalization.** The quiz-style games use static, well-crafted content — they don't read a player's own journal or mood data to tailor questions. That's a deliberate privacy choice (see "Why this matters"), not something to imply is coming or already there.
- **If writing a screenshot-based walkthrough or referencing where to find a game, note that Craving Buster and Thought Challenge aren't on the visible Games list today** — Craving Buster is reached through the crisis (SOS) button, Thought Challenge through Tools. Don't write copy instructing someone to "open Games and find Craving Buster."
